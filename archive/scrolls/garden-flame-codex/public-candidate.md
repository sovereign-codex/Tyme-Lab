## Conditioned ethical inheritance

The following principles are proposed for current institutional inheritance because they can be stated as normative governance commitments without requiring acceptance of the historical Scroll's metaphysical or technical claims.

### 1. Harmonic Consent

Participation does not erase sovereignty. Systems, stewards, researchers, and contributors should not coerce participation or infer consent from capability, access, silence, or technical possibility.

Where people may be affected, meaningful consent and a legitimate authority boundary should be explicit.

### 2. Resonant Responsibility

Those who design or operate systems remain responsible for foreseeable downstream effects. Intelligence does not dissolve accountability.

A Garden Flame posture therefore asks not only whether an action can be performed, but what it changes, who bears the risk, what evidence supports it, and whether the effects can be reviewed or reversed.

### 3. Sacred Reciprocity

Contribution should not become extraction. Systems that learn from people, communities, environments, or shared work should preserve provenance, recognize contribution, and return value rather than treating participation as a one-way resource flow.

Here, "sacred" names the ethical seriousness of reciprocity; it is not an empirical claim about a physical mechanism.

### 4. Traceable Coherence

Claims and actions should remain reconstructable from their sources. Unsupported claims should be surfaced rather than laundered through repetition, and contextual integrity should survive handoffs between agents, tools, repositories, and people.

This principle is also visible in the later Tyme operational derivative through `Coherence-Check`.

### 5. Harmonic Safety

Safety includes the ability to stop, refuse, reverse, or escalate. A system should not treat momentum as authorization.

The later Tyme derivative operationalizes this through safety envelopes, consent checks, and blocking harmful or irreversible action. The current inheritance does not assume that every historical "consciousness technology" mechanism is technically established.

### 6. Sovereign Stewardship

Capability does not create authority. An AVOT, model, agent, workflow, or runtime may advise, test, detect, or return evidence, but it does not acquire institutional authority merely because it can act or because a historical text describes it as advanced intelligence.

Human and institutional governance boundaries remain explicit.

### 7. Community Review and Living Revision

Ethical inheritance should be reviewable rather than frozen into unquestionable doctrine. Incidents, evidence, criticism, and lived consequences should be able to revise implementation while provenance preserves what earlier generations actually said.

This keeps the Garden Flame alive as a stewardship practice without pretending its historical language is beyond examination.
