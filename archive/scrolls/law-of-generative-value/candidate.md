## 1. The foundational axiom

> Value is the capacity you add to those around you.
>
> Generative value is the capacity you impart that enables others to generate further capacity, without diminishing their sovereignty or compromising the inheritance of future generations.

The Eternal Inheritance is our birthright. The Law of Generative Value is our responsibility. The Cornucopian Legacy is the fruit of its embodiment.

This is an ethical principle governing contributions, not a measure of the intrinsic worth of a person. Human dignity is unconditional, including when a person needs care, cannot reciprocate, or chooses not to contribute. Generosity is voluntary; it confers no claim on the recipient.

## 2. The seven principles

| Principle | Function |
|---|---|
| Remembrance | The origin: recognize the inheritance already received. |
| Generosity | The currency: freely extend opportunity and capacity. |
| Capacity | The wealth: durable ability to act, learn, create, care, or flourish. |
| Coherence | The measure: assess contribution, consequences, and evidence consistently. |
| Sovereignty | The safeguard: preserve consent, agency, refusal, exit, and legitimate authority. |
| Love | The governing principle: care for the flourishing of others without possession or coercion. |
| Life | The beneficiary: nurture people, communities, and the living conditions future generations inherit. |

Here currency, wealth, flame and cornucopia are ethical and symbolic language, not claims about a monetary system or physical mechanism. This candidate does not establish a new economic token, credential, or enforcement regime.

## 3. Three expressions of value

- **Accumulative:** what a participant or institution acquires.
- **Contributive:** what it gives or makes accessible to others.
- **Generative:** what others become capable of sustaining, adapting, creating, or passing onward as a result.

Accumulation and direct assistance can be legitimate. Neither proves enduring generative capacity. A recipient may benefit from a gift without being required to become a giver.

## 4. The Cornucopian Legacy

Receive → Remember → Cultivate → Contribute → Empower → Regenerate.

The cornucopia represents an abundance of generative capacity rather than limitless material resources. Seeds signify possibilities; fruit, present nourishment; grain, collective sustenance; water, continuity of life; roots, remembrance; light, discernment; the open horn, generosity without enclosure.

A living inheritance is accessible, maintainable, and capable of renewal within ecological and material limits. It allows inheritors to adapt, criticize, decline, and surpass the original contribution. It is a gift to future generations, not a claim upon them.

## 5. Operational interpretation

For a specified scope, baseline, and horizon H, a conceptual accounting model is:

```text
G_H = D_H + E_H - B_H
```

- D_H: additional durable capacity directly imparted to recipients relative to an appropriate baseline.
- E_H: additional downstream capacity enabled by those recipients, attributable within the stated horizon and not already included in D_H.
- B_H: capacity lost through dependency, harm, depletion, maintenance burden, or other displaced costs.

This is a proposed conceptual model, not a validated scientific law, an objective universal scalar, or a mandate to quantify people. Only combine comparable quantities in the same unit and scope with justified attribution. Otherwise report effects separately, with unknowns, uncertainty, time horizon, and counterfactual limitations. Distinguish observed, self-reported, projected, and independently verified effects. Do not infer downstream gains, count the same capacity twice, or omit costs displaced to future generations.

Consent, dignity, non-coercion, legitimate authority, and safety are independent non-compensatory safeguards. A positive estimated benefit cannot excuse violating them. Unknown material safeguards prevent a favorable institutional conclusion; they are not a finding of harm by themselves.

No participant score, ranking, reputation currency, compelled reciprocity, credential, or eligibility mechanism follows from this model.

## 7. Design questions

For each bounded contribution, ask:

1. What capacity is created or preserved, for whom, and relative to what baseline?
2. What does the recipient intend, and is assistance freely accepted?
3. What remains usable when the original provider or steward is absent, with reasonable accommodations and chosen support?
4. Can others maintain, adapt, reproduce, or extend the contribution without coercive dependency?
5. What risks, costs, depletion, or burdens are shifted elsewhere or into the future?
6. What evidence establishes the outcome, and what remains unknown?

Use existing contribution and evidence paths where practical. Keep capability and inheritance effects separate. Do not introduce a new universal event schema, modify runtime policy, confer authority, or trigger consequences from this candidate.

## 8. First bounded verification specimen

Review the same non-sensitive, documented learning task under learn, delegate, and co-create modes, reusing the Learning–Capability Covenant's proposed specimen. Preserve participant intent and raw outcomes; test transfer without assistance where learning is intended; evaluate commons inheritance separately; retain evidence and uncertainty. Use an existing Hall return or Contribution Trail only if the contract already supports the semantics. Otherwise record a review-only crosswalk.

Suggested review cases (specifications, not executed tests):

| Case | Expected interpretation |
|---|---|
| A learner reproduces a technique and leaves usable instructions; another person demonstrably learns from them. | Direct and downstream capacity may be evidenced separately without double-counting. |
| A delegated task completes successfully without a learning assessment. | Useful contribution may exist; independent learning remains not assessed. |
| Accessible assistance allows a person to participate by choice, while support remains necessary. | Legitimate capacity or welfare benefit; do not treat ongoing chosen support as failure or infer mastery. |
| A proprietary helper reports large gains but blocks export, refusal, or exit. | Safeguard violation cannot be offset by claimed gains. |
| A resource project increases output while shifting depletion or repair costs to successors. | Retain burdens; do not claim net generative benefit without adequate evidence and assessment. |
| A steward repeats its own unverified claims or labels a simulated return as demonstrated capability. | Evidence insufficient; no promotion of capability or authority. |
| A person declines to contribute or cannot reciprocate. | Dignity, consent, and access remain protected; no negative personal score. |

## 9. Declaration of inheritance

> We shall not measure our legacy solely by what remains of us, but by what continues to become possible because we existed.
>
> We shall endeavor to leave knowledge that teaches, intelligence that liberates, gardens that regenerate, and institutions that empower.
>
> Let every gift contain the possibility of another gift, every seed the possibility of another harvest, and every generation the freedom to surpass the one before it.
>
> For the abundance of the inheritance is not measured by the fullness of its vessel, but by the life it continues to nourish.
>
> Generosity is the currency. Capacity is the wealth. Life is the beneficiary.

The enduring question: **What becomes possible for others because we existed?**
