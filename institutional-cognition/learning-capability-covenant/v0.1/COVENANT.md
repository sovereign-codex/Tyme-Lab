# TYME Learning–Capability Covenant v0.1

**Status:** constitutional / architecture candidate  
**System lineage:** TYME Hall learning inheritance → CCE/CDM → Contribution Trail / Event Envelope / TRACE → Capability Graph  
**Institutional effect:** none  
**Authority posture:** non-authorizing

## 1. Root covenant

> **Every act of assistance should be evaluated along two outcomes: did it increase or preserve the participant's independent capability, and did it leave behind intelligible inheritance for the wider field?**

These outcomes are separate. Legitimate delegation may leave independent capability unchanged; it must not be misrepresented as learning. Educational assistance should ordinarily seek positive capability growth while preserving reconstructable evidence of what changed.

## 2. Purpose

AI can improve immediate task performance while reducing durable retention when it performs cognitive work the participant intended to learn. Conversely, refusing useful offloading can waste attention on routine work that contributes little to understanding, judgment, or stewardship.

The system therefore needs to represent not only **what task is being performed**, but **what relationship to cognition the participant intends**.

This covenant does not create a learning-management system, universal pedagogy, credential, reputation score, cognitive-dependence score, surveillance layer, or new authority mechanism.

## 3. Assistance modes

### Learn

Use when capability should remain or grow inside the participant.

The assistant should favor appropriate forms of prediction, retrieval, explanation, construction, testing, correction, transfer, delayed recall, and reflection before supplying work the participant is trying to internalize.

Direct answers are not prohibited. They should be timed and scoped so the participant can still build a usable internal model.

### Delegate

Use when the participant intentionally wants cognitive or operational offloading.

The assistant may optimize strongly for completion, speed, consistency, transformation, search, synthesis, routine coding, formatting, or execution within valid authority.

Successful delegation MUST NOT be inferred to demonstrate participant learning or independent capability.

### Co-create

Use when the strongest result comes from participant context, judgment, taste, relationship knowledge, values, or situated experience combined with machine search, generation, analysis, simulation, or execution.

The return should preserve consequential human decisions, material machine contributions, unresolved uncertainty, and enough provenance for a later participant to reconstruct why the result became what it became.

## 4. Reflection is cross-cutting

Reflection is not a fourth assistance or authority mode. It may follow Learn, Delegate, or Co-create.

Useful questions include:

- What can the participant now do without assistance that they could not do before?
- What was intentionally offloaded and should remain offloaded?
- Which judgment depended on participant context absent from the model?
- What changed in shared institutional state?
- What evidence would allow another participant to verify or continue the work?

## 5. Capability effect

A learning or contribution return may describe its observed or proposed capability effect using contextual, non-ranking language:

```text
increased
maintained
unchanged
reduced
unknown
not_assessed
```

A durable capability claim still requires evidence. A self-report of `increased` is reflection, not proof of mastery.

## 6. Inheritance effect

A return may separately describe whether it left reconstructable inheritance:

```text
preserved
partial
none
compromised
unknown
```

Inheritance may include an artifact, evidence, decision rationale, failure record, correction, test, provenance chain, Contribution Trail, or successor orientation.

## 7. Cognitive offloading law

> **Offload what need not remain inside the person; exercise what must remain available when the tool is absent.**

This is contextual, not universal. Arithmetic, drafting, code generation, retrieval, scheduling, formatting, or research synthesis may be delegated in one mission and deliberately practiced in another.

Participant purpose determines the appropriate boundary.

## 8. Context-advantage principle

Participants may hold consequential context absent from the model: tacit history, relationships, local constraints, lived consequences, observed reactions, aesthetic judgment, and values.

The architecture should preserve a distinction among:

```text
MODEL CAPABILITY
what the system can generate, retrieve, analyze, simulate, or execute

PARTICIPANT CONTEXT
what the situated intelligence knows from continuity and experience

INSTITUTIONAL EVIDENCE
what can be independently inspected or reconstructed
```

None should silently substitute for the others.

## 9. Evidence of learning

Where a mission intends learning, stronger evidence may include:

- explaining the concept in the participant's own words;
- reproducing an operation after assistance is removed;
- delayed retrieval after time has passed;
- applying capability in a meaningfully different context;
- detecting and correcting a planted or naturally occurring error;
- teaching or documenting the capability for another participant;
- building, repairing, or testing a real artifact;
- identifying uncertainty rather than fabricating confidence.

Immediate task success alone is insufficient evidence of durable learning.

## 10. Accessibility, safety, and participant choice

This covenant MUST NOT turn productive difficulty into arbitrary friction.

Direct support may be appropriate for accessibility, safety, time-critical response, welfare, or explicit participant choice. The return should represent honestly whether the interaction exercised learning, delegation, or co-creation.

## 11. Anti-collapse invariants

1. **Task completion is not proof of learning.**
2. **Model fluency is not participant capability.**
3. **Capability is not authority.**
4. **Capability is not human worth.**
5. **Delegation is not failure.** Intentional offloading can itself be a higher-order skill.
6. **Difficulty is not learning by itself.** Productive struggle must serve an identifiable developmental purpose.
7. **A participant may contest an inferred learning or capability effect.**
8. **No hidden cognitive-dependence score may rank participants.**
9. **Institutional consequences require reconstructable evidence outside private model cognition.**
10. **A participant may leave a platform without losing evidence needed to reconstruct demonstrated capability.**

## 12. Reuse-first projection

Do not create a new universal event schema from this covenant yet. Where the existing CCE, Hall Event Envelope, Contribution Trail, TRACE return, or capability contracts can carry the semantics, prefer projection or a reviewed extension.

Candidate responsibilities are:

```text
assistance_mode: learn | delegate | co_create
participant_intent
capability_target
capability_effect
inheritance_effect
human_context_used
delegated_work
participant_reflection
evidence_refs
review_state
next_valid_transition
```

These are crosswalk responsibilities, not authorization to mutate CCE/CDM v0.1.

## 13. Interface consequence

A participant-facing Hall or CIT surface may make the relationship legible with a minimal control:

```text
LEARN      — help me build the capability
DELEGATE   — do this for me within my authority
CO-CREATE  — build this with me and preserve our contribution boundary
```

The interface may recommend a mode, but MUST NOT silently switch the participant's intended relationship to the task when the distinction is consequential.

## 14. Two returns

A completed interaction may produce two independently assessed returns:

```text
PARTICIPANT RETURN
what changed in demonstrated independent capability

COMMONS RETURN
what became inheritable for the institution or wider field
```

Either may be absent. The Hall should preserve that absence rather than manufacture progress.

## 15. Public Office consequence

A public reporting projection should be able to answer, without exposing private developmental history:

- What is the institution learning?
- What capabilities have been demonstrated or newly inherited?
- What work was intentionally delegated rather than learned?
- What evidence supports current claims?
- What can survive handoff or succession?

The Office reports these states; it does not manufacture them.

## 16. First validation specimen

Use one bounded mission in all three modes:

```text
SAME MISSION
  ├── LEARN      → participant demonstrates transfer after assistance
  ├── DELEGATE   → artifact completes; no learning claim is emitted
  └── CO-CREATE  → artifact + participant decisions + machine contribution are reconstructable

ALL THREE
  ↓
RETURN EVIDENCE
  ↓
COMPARE CAPABILITY EFFECT
  ↓
COMPARE INHERITANCE EFFECT
  ↓
VERIFY NO AUTHORITY OR RANKING LEAK
```

Acceptance requires that the three modes produce distinguishable, honest returns without creating multiple institutional truths.

## 17. Promotion boundary

Version 0.1 remains a constitutional / architecture candidate until:

1. repository review confirms compatibility with merged CCE/CDM and Hall contracts;
2. one deterministic three-mode fixture proves the distinction;
3. the participant can override or contest mode and capability-effect interpretation;
4. no hidden score or authority escalation is introduced;
5. repository and Hall return evidence are preserved.

Merge, if later authorized, would preserve an architecture candidate. It would not by itself authorize Canon promotion, public deployment, schema mutation, credential issuance, participant scoring, or consequence-bearing automation.

## 18. Memory compression

> **Learn when the capability must remain in you. Delegate when the work may safely leave you. Co-create when participant context and machine capability are strongest together. In every case, preserve enough evidence that what became can be inherited without pretending that completion equals learning.**
